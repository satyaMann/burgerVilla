## 3. Pattern analysis

The paper is not a simple recall test. Its dominant forms are:

- two- or three-statement questions followed by a code;
- correctly matched pair/count questions;
- one-best-answer conceptual questions;
- assertion/explanation or Statement I/Statement II reasoning;
- short numerical cases requiring one to three linked steps;
- data-sufficiency and arrangement problems; and
- passage-based inference rather than factual extraction.

Distractors typically use a true statement placed in the wrong context, reverse a technical relationship, change a statutory number, or exploit a common computational shortcut. The mock follows these distractor principles.

## 4. Difficulty and cognitive demand

The original is **predominantly moderate**, with easier factual/concept-definition items interspersed with harder legal-detail, psychometric and multi-step quantitative items. Difficulty comes more from breadth and close alternatives than from long calculations. The mock uses the same profile:

- **Easy to lower-moderate:** direct definitions, basic institutional roles, elementary central tendency.
- **Moderate:** statement-code policy questions, grammar matching, weighted calculations, validity/reliability distinctions.
- **Upper-moderate to hard:** data sufficiency, constrained arrangements, item analysis, Spearman–Brown calculations and fine statutory distinctions.

This is a qualitative calibration because the source booklet supplies no official item statistics.

## 5. Quality controls applied to the mock

- Every stem is newly authored; some intentionally retain the original item model while using different data, applications or reasoning demands.
- The formal 20/100 part distribution and section-level question counts are retained; related quantitative subtopics are redistributed within Q21–Q40.
- Answer positions are balanced across the complete paper without changing item content or difficulty.
- Numerical answers were independently recomputed from the stated data.
- Statutory and policy items use official primary sources (Government of India, India Code, Parliament, UNICEF/OHCHR, NCERT, NIOS and CBSE).
- Time-sensitive trivia was avoided where a stable policy, statutory or institutional fact could test the same topic.
- Hindi and English Part B versions convey the same proposition and data.
